\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:evaluation-discussion}

\subsection{Answers to the Evaluation Questions}
\label{subsec:evaluation-question-answers}

For EQ1, the complete pipeline changes the strategy materially: it executes 440 trades compared with 578 for the engine-only branch, and its saved results have a smaller mean maximum drawdown together with modestly higher median return, geometric aggregate return, mean and median win rates, and median trade-level Sharpe statistic. Mean return remains slightly lower. It exceeds engine-only return for 16 of 30 symbols and buy-and-hold return for only 3 of 30. Because the full branch changes both candidate acceptance and trade management, these differences describe the combined Buy-or-Wait and recommendation-generated trading-plan layer rather than filtering alone. The evidence does not establish a broadly superior return strategy.

For EQ2, robustness is limited. Results vary substantially among securities, and no individual technical indicator dominates every measure in the regenerated companion ablations. MACD and Bollinger Bands have positive median per-symbol returns, while every indicator has a positive mean return because extreme winners raise the cross-sectional averages. High-confidence trades have a higher win rate but a lower mean return than the smaller medium-confidence group, so confidence is not monotonic across both outcomes. No accepted trade in the saved artifact has confidence below 0.55, and no downtrend trade is present. The first 60 benchmark sessions are also assigned to the sideways regime because a 60-session return is unavailable. These findings do not show that behavior is stable across confidence levels, securities, or market regimes.

For EQ3, only two of 29 evaluable per-symbol one-sample $t$-tests and one stored sign-permutation result are significant at the 5\% level. The score--return information coefficient is weakly negative among 29 evaluable securities (median $-0.051$) and significant for only one before multiple-comparison adjustment. These tests concern returns relative to zero; the current artifacts contain no paired inferential test of the full pipeline against engine-only or buy-and-hold results, no universe-level test, and no multiple-comparison correction. Sparse trades also create unstable risk statistics. The experiment is useful as a transparent descriptive comparison, but it does not establish statistically reliable alpha, superiority to the alternatives, or expected future profitability.

\subsection{Practical Efficiency for Financial Analysis}
\label{subsec:practical-analysis-efficiency}

Although the historical evidence does not support a claim of superior investment returns, Stockrium can still reduce the amount of manual preparation required for an initial stock review. Without the application, this preparation may include locating financial statements, interpreting profitability, liquidity, leverage, valuation, and growth measures, inspecting technical indicators, reading related news, and combining the findings into one assessment.

Stockrium automates much of this preliminary retrieval, calculation, and synthesis. Its specialist analytical nodes retrieve and summarize fundamental, technical, and article evidence, while the recommendation and aggregator stages organize the results into scores, a decision, a trading plan when applicable, confidence, and a structured explanation.

The generated report can therefore serve as a structured starting point and may allow users to focus on validating assumptions, investigating unusual results, and applying judgment. Demonstrating an actual time saving requires a future controlled task study with representative users, identical evidence, and measured completion time and quality. The application should not be interpreted as eliminating source verification or replacing a qualified analyst. The experimental limitations identified below, particularly point-in-time fundamental availability and model nondeterminism, mean that every recommendation still requires human review before an investment decision is made.

The most important data limitation is point-in-time fundamental availability. Technical and article inputs are bounded by the candidate date, and the fundamental branch conservatively excludes annual rows from the simulated year and future years. However, a financial year is not the same as its public filing date. During the early months of a simulated year, the preceding annual record may not yet have been public even though the year-level filter admits it. Single-indicator experiments avoid this publication-timing uncertainty because they do not use fundamental or news evidence, although they retain universe, execution, and portfolio limitations.

\subsection{Overall Evaluation}
\label{subsec:overall-evaluation}

The evaluation demonstrates that Stockrium can produce inspectable historical decisions, compare the complete pipeline descriptively with deterministic and passive alternatives, and expose performance and risk trade-offs. The saved full-pipeline configuration is associated with fewer completed trades and a smaller mean drawdown than the engine-only configuration, but the comparator does not separate the effects of the decision rule from those of recommendation-generated trade management. The results also do not demonstrate consistent benchmark outperformance, strong confidence calibration, or stable behavior across regimes.

The defensible conclusion is therefore limited: Stockrium provides a useful and transparent evaluation environment for Vietnamese equity decision support, while stronger point-in-time data controls, reproducible experiment manifests, realistic portfolio simulation, and larger out-of-sample evidence are required before investment-performance claims can be made.
