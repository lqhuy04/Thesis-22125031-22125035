\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:evaluation-discussion}

\subsection{Answers to the Evaluation Questions}
\label{subsec:evaluation-question-answers}

For EQ1, the agentic AI workflow changes the strategy materially by reducing 756 engine-only trades to 272 executed trades. This filtering lowers mean maximum drawdown and slightly improves the median trade-level Sharpe statistic. However, it also lowers mean and median return, geometric aggregate return, and win rate. It exceeds engine-only return for 14 of 30 symbols and buy-and-hold return for 6 of 30. The evidence therefore supports interpreting the agentic AI stage as a conservative exposure filter, not as a broadly superior return strategy.

For EQ2, robustness is limited. Results vary substantially among securities, no individual technical indicator dominates every measure, and medium-confidence trades outperform the larger high-confidence group rather than following a monotonic confidence pattern. The Buy gate leaves no low-confidence trade group, while the downtrend regime contains only one trade. These findings do not show that the observed behavior is stable across confidence levels, securities, or market regimes.

For EQ3, only two per-symbol one-sample $t$-tests and two sign-permutation tests are significant at the 5\% level, and the score--return information coefficient is near zero (median $-0.034$) and significant for only two securities before multiple-comparison adjustment. No multiple-comparison-adjusted universe-level hypothesis test is stored. Sparse trades also create unstable risk statistics and extreme small-sample correlations. The current experiment is valuable as a transparent comparative evaluation, but it does not establish statistically reliable alpha or expected future profitability.

\subsection{Practical Efficiency for Financial Analysis}
\label{subsec:practical-analysis-efficiency}

Although the historical evidence does not support a claim of superior investment returns, Stockrium still provides an important practical benefit by reducing the time required to prepare an initial stock analysis. A user without a financial background must first locate the relevant financial statements, learn the formulas and meanings of indicators such as profitability, liquidity, leverage, valuation, and growth ratios, calculate or compare the values, inspect price-based technical indicators, read related news, and combine the findings into one interpretation. Completing this process carefully for one security can reasonably occupy most of a working day, especially when the user must learn the analytical concepts at the same time.

An experienced securities analyst can perform the same work more efficiently because the formulas, warning signs, and information sources are already familiar. Nevertheless, collecting current records, checking financial-statement trends, reviewing technical conditions and news, and writing a coherent assessment may still require approximately one to two hours for a single symbol. Repeating the process across several candidate securities causes this preparation time to increase almost linearly.

Stockrium automates much of this preliminary work. Its specialist analytical nodes retrieve and summarize fundamental, technical, and article evidence, while the aggregator organizes the results into scores, confidence, risks, and a structured recommendation. In the 20-concurrent-user system test, full-analysis latency at the 95th percentile was 12.61 seconds off-session and 13.42 seconds while the live market collectors were active (Table~\ref{tab:performance-results}). The comparison is illustrative rather than a controlled productivity experiment, but it shows the system's principal efficiency contribution: work that may require hours of manual collection, formula review, calculation, and synthesis can be reduced to an initial report produced within seconds.

This time saving is most valuable as decision support. The generated report gives inexperienced users a structured starting point and allows experts to spend less time on repetitive data preparation and more time validating assumptions, investigating unusual results, and applying professional judgment. It should not be interpreted as eliminating the need to verify source data or as replacing a qualified analyst. The experimental limitations identified below, particularly point-in-time fundamental availability and model nondeterminism, mean that every recommendation still requires human review before an investment decision is made.

The most important limitation is point-in-time fundamental availability. Technical and article inputs are bounded by the candidate date, but the fundamental branch reads the latest stored multi-year series. The full-pipeline results can therefore contain information unavailable at the simulated decision date. Single-indicator experiments avoid this specific leakage because they do not use fundamental or news evidence, although they retain universe and execution-model limitations.

\subsection{Overall Evaluation}
\label{subsec:overall-evaluation}

The evaluation demonstrates that Stockrium can produce inspectable historical decisions, compare the agentic AI workflow with deterministic and passive alternatives, and expose both performance and risk trade-offs. The agentic AI workflow reduces trading frequency and drawdown, but the saved results do not demonstrate consistent benchmark outperformance, strong confidence calibration, or stable behavior across regimes.

The defensible conclusion is therefore limited: Stockrium provides a useful and transparent evaluation environment for Vietnamese equity decision support, while stronger point-in-time data controls, reproducible experiment manifests, realistic portfolio simulation, and larger out-of-sample evidence are required before investment-performance claims can be made.
