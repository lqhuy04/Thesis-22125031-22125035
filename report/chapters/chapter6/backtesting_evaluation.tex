\section{Historical Backtesting Evaluation}
\label{sec:historical-backtesting-evaluation}

\subsection{VN30 Experimental Configuration}
\label{subsec:backtest-vn30-configuration}

Two saved experiment groups are available. The complete agentic AI pipeline report contains 30 symbols and was accumulated between July~18 and July~20, 2026. The requested daily interval is January~1, 2023 through December~31, 2025, with a 20-candle maximum hold and the default cost of 0.15\% per side. The artifact stores full-pipeline and engine-only performance, buy-and-hold and random comparisons, statistical tests, regime results, and confidence calibration. It is updated one symbol at a time rather than written from one atomic batch, so the per-symbol completion timestamps span the full execution period.

The second group isolates each technical indicator. These ablations use the 30 symbols returned by the current VN30 lookup, daily data from January~1, 2023 through December~31, 2025, a 20-candle maximum hold, no language-model calls, and the default cost of 0.15\% per side. Each binary indicator score replaces the combined score, and every eligible date with score one creates a candidate. All 30 symbols completed each of the five ablations.

The universe is the VN30 list available when the experiments were executed, not a date-reconstructed historical membership list. The results therefore describe the selected large-cap stock set and are not a survivorship-bias-free replication of the index.

The full pipeline admitted 64.0\% fewer trades and reduced mean maximum drawdown by 9.93 percentage points. Its median trade-level Sharpe statistic was modestly higher, but its mean and median per-symbol returns, geometric aggregate return, and mean and median win rates were lower. The pipeline exceeded engine-only return for 14 of 30 symbols and buy-and-hold return for 6 of 30. Passive buy-and-hold remains the strongest same-security reference: its cross-sectional mean return of 99.25\% and median of 78.61\% exceed the pipeline's 25.66\% mean and 12.31\% median, because the pipeline is invested only during selected intervals rather than continuously through the 2023--2025 uptrend. The saved evidence therefore characterizes the agentic AI workflow primarily as a conservative exposure filter rather than a source of broad return outperformance. These full-pipeline figures must be read together with the point-in-time limitation in Section~\ref{subsec:backtest-validity-limitations}: only the full pipeline uses the fundamental branch, whose latest-record loading can introduce look-ahead, so the cleanest leakage-free evidence is the single-indicator ablation reported in Section~\ref{subsec:backtest-indicator-ablation-results}.

The stored mean pipeline Sharpe statistic is 0.271, compared with a median of 0.746. The per-symbol estimates remain sensitive to sparse observations: every symbol has fewer than 30 pipeline trades, six have fewer than five, and SHB's highest reported Sharpe statistic of 4.130 is based on only three trades. Because the trade-level Sharpe statistic scales with the square root of the trade count (Equation~\ref{eq:backtest-sharpe}) and the pipeline executes fewer trades per symbol than the engine, this scaling works against the pipeline; the higher median pipeline Sharpe is therefore not an artifact of a larger sample. HDB has the highest saved pipeline total return at 164.47\%, while GVR has the lowest at $-35.87$\%, demonstrating substantial cross-sectional variation.

Only two of the 30 one-sample $t$-tests and two sign-permutation tests are significant at the 5\% level. The median random-entry percentile is 71.45\%, but no multiple-comparison-adjusted universe-level test is present. Most individual results therefore do not reject a zero-mean or random-timing explanation, and the evidence does not establish statistically reliable alpha.

The score--return information coefficient reinforces this caution. Correlating the technical score at trade entry with the realized trade return gives a mean information coefficient of 0.011 and a median of $-0.034$ across the 30 symbols; the coefficient is positive for 12 securities, negative for 16, flat for one, and unavailable for the zero-trade MSN result. Only two coefficients are significant at the unadjusted 5\% level. The most extreme coefficient, $-1.000$ for VPL, comes from only two trades and has a $p$-value of 1.0; HPG's significant coefficient of 0.978 is likewise based on only four trades. The entry score therefore exhibits, at best, a weak and statistically unreliable monotonic relationship with subsequent return, which is consistent with interpreting the pipeline as an exposure filter rather than a return-ranking signal.

Regime grouping assigns 232 trades to sideways conditions, 39 to uptrends, and only one to a downtrend. Sideways trades record a 47.41\% win rate and 2.75\% mean return; uptrend trades record a 30.77\% win rate and 1.21\% mean return. The single downtrend trade loses 6.62\%, but one observation cannot support a meaningful regime conclusion.

Bollinger conditions produce the highest mean per-symbol return, while KDJ produces the highest mean Sharpe statistic and win rate. Moving-average conditions generate approximately half as many trades as the reactive oscillators and have the smallest mean drawdown, but also the lowest mean return. No indicator dominates every criterion. This supports using complementary technical perspectives, but it does not prove that equal binary weighting is optimal.
