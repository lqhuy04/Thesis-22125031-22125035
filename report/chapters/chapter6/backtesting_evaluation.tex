\section{Historical Backtesting Evaluation}
\label{sec:historical-backtesting-evaluation}

\subsection{VN30 Experimental Configuration}
\label{subsec:backtest-vn30-configuration}

Two saved experiment groups are available. The latest complete multi-agent pipeline report contains the 30 symbols returned by the current VN30 lookup and was finalized on August~2, 2026. The requested daily interval is August~1, 2024 through August~1, 2026, and the default transaction cost is 0.15\% per side. The request-level holding limit is 20 candles. Engine-only trades use that limit, while full-pipeline trades use a valid recommendation-generated holding override when present and otherwise fall back to 20; they also use recommendation-generated take-profit and stop-loss levels. The artifact stores full-pipeline and engine-only performance, buy-and-hold comparisons, statistical tests, regime results, and confidence calibration. It is updated one symbol at a time rather than written from one atomic batch, and its per-symbol completion timestamps span August~1 through August~2, 2026.

The second group isolates each technical indicator. These ablations use the same 30 symbols, requested daily interval, 20-candle hold, and 0.15\% per-side cost, without language-model calls. Before the evaluation boundary is applied, the runner loads earlier daily history, calculates all indicators, and preserves the shared 50-candle warm-up; it then selects one binary score and trims to August~1, 2024. Every eligible date with score one creates a candidate. All 30 symbols completed each of the five regenerated ablations without a recorded error, and every artifact records a 50-candle warm-up and pre-start history loading.

The universe is the 30-symbol result returned by the current VN30 lookup, not a date-reconstructed historical membership list. The full-pipeline and all five single-indicator artifacts use this same symbol set. The results therefore describe the selected large-cap stock set and are not a survivorship-bias-free replication of the index.

The full pipeline executed 23.9\% fewer trades and had a mean maximum drawdown 2.18 percentage points closer to zero. Its median trade-level Sharpe statistic, median per-symbol return, geometric aggregate return, and mean and median win rates were modestly higher than the engine-only baseline, while mean return was slightly lower. The pipeline had 22 profitable symbols compared with 21 for the engine, exceeded engine-only return for 16 of 30 symbols, and exceeded buy-and-hold return for 3 of 30. Passive buy-and-hold remains the stronger same-security reference across the selected universe: its cross-sectional mean return of 76.38\% and median of 36.86\% exceed the pipeline's 34.71\% mean and 20.30\% median, although the pipeline is invested only during selected intervals and incurs the simulator's transaction-cost deduction. These differences describe the combined Buy-or-Wait and recommendation-generated trade-management layer; they cannot be attributed to candidate filtering alone. The remaining fundamental point-in-time concern is narrower than unrestricted latest-record loading: the backtest excludes same-year and future annual rows, but the data lack filing-availability timestamps, so prior-year records may be treated as available before their actual publication date.

The stored mean pipeline Sharpe statistic is 0.661, compared with a median of 0.604. The per-symbol estimates remain sensitive to sparse observations: every symbol has fewer than 30 pipeline trades, and VPL has only one. Because the trade-level Sharpe statistic scales with the square root of the trade count (Equation~\ref{eq:backtest-sharpe}) and the pipeline executes fewer trades per symbol than the engine, this scaling works against the pipeline; its higher median Sharpe is therefore not an artifact of a larger sample. VIC has both the highest saved pipeline total return, 281.93\%, and the highest Sharpe statistic, 2.740, based on 19 trades; VNM has the lowest total return at $-25.82$\%. This spread demonstrates substantial cross-sectional variation.

Only two of the 29 evaluable one-sample $t$-tests and one stored sign-permutation result are significant at the 5\% level; VPL has only one trade, so its $t$-test is not informative. No multiple-comparison correction, aggregate universe-level test, or paired inferential comparison against engine-only or buy-and-hold performance is present. Most individual results therefore do not reject a zero-mean explanation, and the evidence does not establish statistically reliable alpha or statistically demonstrated superiority to those alternatives.

The score--return information coefficient reinforces this caution. Across the 29 securities with an evaluable coefficient, correlating the signal-date technical score associated with each trade with its realized return gives a mean coefficient of $-0.060$ and a median of $-0.051$; 12 coefficients are positive and 17 are negative. VPL is excluded from these summaries because its single trade yields insufficient data rather than a flat relationship. Only VPB is significant at the unadjusted 5\% level, and its coefficient is negative ($-0.509$, $p=0.037$). The technical entry signal therefore exhibits a weak and statistically unreliable monotonic relationship with subsequent return.

\subsection{Confidence and Regime Results}
\label{subsec:backtest-robustness-results}

Table~\ref{tab:backtest-confidence-results} pools confidence-tier outcomes. High-confidence trades have the higher win rate, while medium-confidence trades have the higher mean net return and contain only 30 observations. The saved artifact happens to contain no accepted trade below 0.55 confidence. This absence is not caused by the Buy gate: the deterministic recommendation is decided from component scores before the aggregator generates confidence. Because returns are stored only for accepted Buy trades, the decision-conditioned sample cannot provide calibration across all candidates or support a monotonic confidence claim.

Regime grouping assigns 364 trades to sideways conditions and 76 to uptrends, with no executed downtrend trades. Sideways trades record a 51.65\% win rate and 2.02\% mean return; uptrend trades record a 47.37\% win rate and 2.47\% mean return. The absence of downtrend observations prevents any claim about performance in falling markets. In addition, the benchmark frame begins at the requested evaluation boundary, so its first 60 sessions have no 60-session return and are assigned to the sideways group by implementation; the sideways result therefore includes an initialization effect.

MA conditions produce the highest mean per-symbol return and the smallest mean drawdown, but their mean return is heavily influenced by VIC's 770.68\% result; the corresponding median is $-1.20$\%. MACD has the highest median total return (4.95\%) and mean Sharpe statistic, while Bollinger Bands also has a slightly positive median return (0.83\%). KDJ has the highest mean win rate and largest trade count, whereas MA produces the fewest trades. No indicator dominates every criterion, and the gaps between mean and median returns show that a small number of securities still raise the positive cross-sectional means. This supports reporting complementary perspectives and robust summaries, but it does not prove that equal binary weighting is optimal.
