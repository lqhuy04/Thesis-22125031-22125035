\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Research related to Stockrium lies at the intersection of financial decision support, large language models, multi-agent systems, financial-news sentiment analysis, and commercial brokerage platforms in Vietnam. This section reviews representative studies and platforms in these areas and identifies the gap addressed by the proposed application.

\subsection{LLM-Based Agent Systems for Financial Decision Support}

LLM-based agent systems organize financial reasoning through specialized roles, memory structures, or both. TradingAgents is a representative multi-agent framework that models a trading firm through analyst agents, Bull and Bear researchers, a trader, and a risk-management team \cite{xiao2024tradingagents}. The authors report that this structured collaboration combines heterogeneous evidence and improves selected return and risk measures. Its multi-round debates nevertheless require repeated model calls and can therefore increase latency and inference cost, while the final action remains strongly dependent on the language model's reasoning path. FinMem instead equips a single trading agent with layered memory that retrieves financial information according to temporal relevance \cite{yu2023finmem}, highlighting the value of maintaining context across changing market conditions, although its main objective remains autonomous trading performance. Stockrium adopts role specialization within a bounded multi-agent graph designed for Vietnamese equities: technical, fundamental, and news analytical agents operate in parallel, after which their evidence is synthesized. Deterministic scoring and validation are added around the LLM output to improve traceability and constrain the final recommendation. Thus, Stockrium is positioned as a user-facing decision-support system rather than an autonomous trading agent or a debate-based simulation of a financial institution.

\subsection{Finance-Specific Large Language Models}

Finance-specific language models show that domain data and adaptation can improve the interpretation of specialized terminology, disclosures, and market communication. BloombergGPT is a 50-billion-parameter BLOOM-style model trained from scratch on a mixed corpus comprising extensive financial data and general-purpose text; its evaluation indicates that this domain-focused pretraining improves performance on financial natural-language-processing tasks while retaining general capabilities \cite{wu2023bloomberggpt}. FinGPT instead presents an open-source, data-centric framework centered on automated financial-data curation and lightweight low-rank adaptation of available foundation models for applications such as sentiment analysis, robo-advising, and algorithmic trading \cite{yang2023fingpt}. The two projects therefore use different development strategies and should not both be characterized simply as fine-tuned models. Stockrium's current implementation uses general-purpose hosted models within a controlled application workflow, where structured data processing and deterministic rules remain responsible for calculations and output validation while language-model nodes interpret and synthesize prepared evidence. As sufficient Vietnamese market and news data become available, evaluating a finance-specific model or an adapted foundation model for the same analytical roles is a viable future direction.

\subsection{Financial News Sentiment Analysis}

Financial news may contain events and expectations that are not fully reflected in historical prices, but its specialized vocabulary limits the effectiveness of general sentiment models. FinBERT addresses this problem through domain-specific pretraining and fine-tuning, achieving improved positive, neutral, and negative classification on financial datasets \cite{araci2019finbert}. FinBERT-LSTM subsequently combined news sentiment with historical prices for NASDAQ-100 prediction, showing that textual information can supplement numerical time series \cite{halder2022finbertlstm}; however, it remains a price-prediction pipeline and does not reconcile news with fundamental and technical evidence. Vietnamese studies provide more locally relevant findings. Tung et al. reported that PhoBERT outperformed conventional models on Vietnamese stock-article titles, reaching approximately 93\% accuracy \cite{tung2021phobert}. Using a year-long collection from three Vietnamese financial-news platforms, Vu et al. achieved over 81\% accuracy in binary positive--negative sentiment classification \cite{vu2023sentiments}. Their event study found significant changes in the return variance of the VN30 and HNX30 indices around negative news over the examined 30-day windows, but no statistically significant changes in mean returns. These results suggest that sentiment can provide useful contextual and risk information but should not independently determine an investment action. Stockrium therefore summarizes and assesses stock-related news as one analytical source whose output is combined with technical and fundamental evidence.

\subsection{Commercial Platforms}

Commercial brokerage platforms in Vietnam, including SSI iBoard Pro, DNSE, and VNDIRECT, combine market information and trading functions with platform-specific analytical and investment-support tools. In particular, SSI iBoard Pro provides research reports and recommendations curated by SSI analysts, recommendation-performance tracking, and a paper-trading facility \cite{ssiIboardResearch2026,ssiIboardLaunch2023}. Although their feature sets differ, these commercial platforms primarily deliver analysis and services within their respective brokerage ecosystems rather than exposing a configurable, automated workflow that separately scores and synthesizes technical, fundamental, and Vietnamese-news evidence. Stockrium is differentiated more narrowly by its automated multi-agent processing: it exposes separate technical, fundamental, and Vietnamese-news outputs, converts them into explicit per-source scores, supports user-configurable weights, applies deterministic Buy-or-Wait constraints, and evaluates the same supported pipeline through an internal backtest. This distinction concerns the implemented method of evidence synthesis and evaluation, not a claim that existing brokerage platforms provide no analytical support.

\subsection{Research Gap and Positioning of Stockrium}

The reviewed research and commercial platforms reveal a separation among key capabilities: sentiment studies classify news, finance-specific LLMs address language understanding, multi-agent frameworks often target autonomous trading in international markets, and brokerage platforms deliver analysis within their own service ecosystems. None of the reviewed works or platforms combines technical, fundamental, and Vietnamese news evidence in a user-facing application while also supporting configurable analysis, investment-horizon awareness, explainable recommendations, deterministic decision constraints, and internal backtesting. Stockrium is designed to address this integration gap. Its explicit multi-agent workflow coordinates specialized analytical agents, allows users to select analytical inputs and their relative importance, and uses an LLM to interpret and synthesize evidence rather than perform every calculation. Deterministic components calculate indicators and weighted scores, enforce the Buy-or-Wait thresholds, and validate trading-plan constraints; the aggregator subsequently generates a bounded confidence assessment and explanation. The internal backtesting facility enables Stockrium's analytical pipeline and supported configurations to be evaluated on historical Vietnamese data. The contribution is therefore not a new forecasting model or a claim of guaranteed superior returns; it is the construction of a localized, configurable, and transparent decision-support environment that brings previously separate analytical capabilities into a coherent system.
