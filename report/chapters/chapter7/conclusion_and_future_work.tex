\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:chapter7-conclusion}

This thesis presented Stockrium, an integrated multi-agent decision-support and research platform for the Vietnamese equity market. The work was motivated by a practical information problem: an individual investor must often combine price behavior, company fundamentals, and financial news while navigating data from separate sources and interpreting results without a consistent analytical framework. Stockrium addresses this problem through a mobile application, a layered backend, a coordinated multi-agent workflow, structured data-acquisition pipelines, and a historical backtesting subsystem. The system is designed to organize evidence and explain an assessment; it does not replace investor judgment or execute trades.

The implemented architecture separates presentation, domain services, data storage, deterministic financial computation, and language-model reasoning. Within the multi-agent workflow, technical, fundamental, and article agents examine different evidence domains. The recommendation stage deterministically applies weighted component scores and technical acceptance thresholds, while the aggregator generates a bounded confidence value and structured explanation without changing the established Buy-or-Wait decision. This design makes individual evidence contributions visible, allows analytical branches to be enabled or disabled, and supports reuse of the same logic in interactive analysis and historical evaluation.

The historical experiment provides a deliberately cautious view of the analytical results. On the current 30-security VN30 stock set, evaluated from August~1, 2024 to August~1, 2026, the complete pipeline executed 440 trades compared with 578 for the engine-only branch. It had a smaller mean maximum drawdown and modestly higher median return, geometric aggregate return, mean and median win rates, and median trade-level Sharpe statistic, while mean return was slightly lower. It outperformed the engine-only result for 16 of 30 securities and buy-and-hold for only 3 of 30. Because the full branch changes both candidate acceptance and trade management, the differences cannot be attributed to filtering alone. Statistical and robustness evidence remained limited, and the results do not establish reliable market outperformance or guaranteed future profit.

The first objective was achieved through the completed Stockrium application and its supporting backend services. Stockrium provides a coherent path from market discovery to evidence inspection and AI-assisted synthesis. Its configurable analysis supports both a simplified automatic mode and a manual mode in which analytical perspectives and weights can be selected. The structured response exposes component scores, confidence, explanations, and risk-related price levels when a Buy recommendation is accepted. These implemented capabilities satisfy the objective of providing individual investors with an integrated and inspectable decision-support system. As intended in the objective, the system supports investor judgment rather than claiming that every recommendation is correct or replacing professional financial advice.

The second objective was achieved as a complete engineering and evaluation capability. The backtesting subsystem reuses the production multi-agent logic, indicator service, deterministic reference-signal module, model-backed technical analysis, and horizon-aware recommendation controls; it prevents same-close execution, includes a warm-up interval, applies an explicit fixed transaction cost, and records full-pipeline and engine-only results. Sharing the implementation removes the drift risk created by separately maintained agent-level and historical calculations. The completed experiment demonstrates that authorized evaluators can run the supported pipeline on historical data, compare configurations and benchmarks, and inspect performance and risk measures. The objective concerns the availability and operation of this evaluation tool, not proof of profitable performance.

The third objective was achieved by providing a curated and consistently structured dataset across the three evidence domains required by Stockrium. The acquisition pipelines collect and normalize historical price and volume observations, company profiles and financial records, industry aggregates, and Vietnamese financial articles with generated summaries and sentiment labels. Validation and transformation steps improve consistency and accuracy, while scheduled and maintainable collection processes keep the available data current. The resulting dataset already supports the application's analysis and historical evaluation functions and provides a comprehensive foundation that can be reused and extended in subsequent research and applications. A versioned public benchmark may be developed in the future, but it is beyond the scope of the objective defined in this thesis.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}

\subsection{Backtesting System Improvements}

The current backtesting system simulates capital independently for each security. In the present implementation, every security is assigned its own capital balance, so simultaneous positions do not compete for the same available cash. Future work should introduce shared capital allocation across securities. A portfolio-level execution model will track a common cash balance, position sizing, concurrent-exposure limits, and portfolio-level risk controls.

The simulation simplifies several conditions encountered in real-world trading. In particular, the current execution model does not fully represent price gaps, Vietnamese price limits, market liquidity, bid--ask spreads, or slippage. Future work should incorporate more realistic trading and execution assumptions. The simulator will model gap-aware fills, enforce applicable price limits, and estimate the effects of liquidity, spreads, and slippage on executed trades.

The current evaluation is limited in both market coverage and duration. Specifically, the experiment evaluates 30 VN30 securities from August~1, 2024 to August~1, 2026, which provides only a relatively short observation period. Future work should extend the evaluation period and broaden the security universe. Subsequent experiments will cover the VN100 or, where sufficient data are available, all securities listed on HOSE, HNX, and UPCOM across longer and more varied market conditions.

\subsection{Analytical and Multi-Agent Improvements}

The current analysis results lack direct citations to the evidence used in their reasoning. Specifically, exact candlesticks, financial periods, ratio values, and supporting articles are not attached to the corresponding claims, making direct verification difficult. Future work should make every analytical claim traceable to its supporting evidence. Each specialist agent will cite the relevant candlesticks, reporting periods, ratios, and articles in its output so that users can inspect them directly.

The multi-agent workflow cannot fully eliminate reasoning errors or unsupported conclusions. In the current workflow, the aggregator synthesizes specialist outputs but does not systematically reject every unsupported claim or restart an analysis when confidence is low. Future work should strengthen validation at the aggregation stage. The aggregator agent will verify claims against cited evidence, reject claims without adequate support, and request a new analysis whenever the resulting confidence is below an acceptable threshold.

Human verification remains necessary to ensure the reliability of investment recommendations. At present, Stockrium's analytical results have not undergone broad and systematic review by investment experts. Future work should incorporate expert review into the evaluation and improvement process. Investment professionals will assess factual grounding, reasoning, explanation quality, and practical usefulness, and their feedback will be used to refine the analytical workflow.

\subsection{System Quality and User Experience}

The application depends on external market-data providers, storage infrastructure, and language-model services. In the current system, an outage, quota restriction, response-format change, or behavioral change in one of these services can interrupt data retrieval or AI-assisted analysis. Future work should make the application more resilient to external-service failures. The system will introduce fallback providers, caching mechanisms, service-health monitoring, and graceful error handling to preserve essential functionality when a dependency is unavailable.

The data displayed in the application do not currently update continuously in real time. Market information is loaded through request--response operations and remains unchanged on the screen until the application performs another data request. Future work should integrate real-time market-data streaming into the mobile application. WebSocket subscriptions will update stock prices and market information continuously and will include reconnection, resubscription, heartbeat handling, and stale-data indicators.

Validation of security, load handling, system recovery, and usability remains limited. The current prototype has not undergone comprehensive testing in these areas. Future work should conduct broader technical and user-centered validation before production-scale deployment. This validation will include security assessments, load and stress tests, failure-recovery exercises, and systematic usability studies.

\section{Closing Remarks}
\label{sec:closing-remarks}

Stockrium demonstrates that heterogeneous Vietnamese market evidence can be organized into a coherent mobile and research platform and processed through an inspectable combination of deterministic analytics and specialized language-model reasoning nodes. Its strongest result is not a claim of superior returns, but the construction of an end-to-end environment in which analytical assumptions, decisions, baselines, and limitations can be examined together.

The evaluation also shows why caution is essential in financial AI. A system may generate structured explanations and reduce exposure while still failing to outperform simple alternatives consistently. By reporting this distinction and identifying the data and experimental controls still required, the thesis establishes a practical foundation for future work on more reproducible, realistic, and trustworthy decision support for the Vietnamese stock market.
