\section{Application Idea}
\label{sec:application-idea}

Stockrium is proposed as a decision-support platform that helps individual investors examine Vietnamese listed companies without collecting information from many disconnected sources. The mobile application guides the user through a continuous process of discovering a stock, reviewing market and company information, configuring an analysis, receiving a multi-perspective assessment, and investigating the supporting evidence. Technical, fundamental, and news perspectives remain distinguishable so that the recommendation is presented as an explainable synthesis rather than an unexplained prediction.

The project also provides an internal experimentation and backtesting web interface for controlled analysis and historical evaluation, together with a curated dataset covering price and volume history, company fundamentals, and financial news with sentiment labels. Stockrium remains analytical rather than transactional: it does not place securities orders, transfer funds, or connect to brokerage firms. Any investment action following an analysis remains the user's responsibility.

\subsection{Stakeholders}
\label{subsec:application-stakeholders}

The proposed system considers four principal stakeholder categories. Individual investors are the primary users, while an authorized evaluator conducts controlled experiments through the restricted web interface. A securities analyst contributes practical evaluation, and technical teams and service providers support the data and software infrastructure.

\subsection{Main Application Flows}
\label{subsec:main-application-flows}

The use cases form two primary experiences: an investor-facing mobile journey and an experimentation and backtesting workflow. These flows describe user-visible behavior and intentionally treat the internal multi-agent workflow as a single analytical service; its agents, state, and execution logic are presented in Chapter 4.

\subsubsection{Mobile Exploration and Stock-Analysis Flow}

A guest first completes onboarding and authenticates through registration, password sign-in, or the supported Google Sign-In flow. After authentication, the investor can enter a stock context from the market overview, search, financial news, or the watchlist. The stock-detail view consolidates price and volume charts, company information, fundamental data, technical indicators, and related news, allowing the investor to examine the available evidence before requesting an assessment.

The user may proceed with an automatic analysis configuration or manually select the evidence sources and their relative weights. The request combines the selected configuration, stock symbol, and saved investment horizon. Figure~\ref{fig:stockrium-primary-flow} summarizes the first part of the mobile journey, from account access and stock discovery to submission of the analysis request.

After reviewing the recommendation, the investor can return to the supporting charts and company data, revise the configuration, or update the watchlist. These paths form an iterative cycle in which the user continues to examine the evidence rather than acting solely on the generated action label.

\subsubsection{Conversational Assistant Flow}

The conversational assistant supports independent financial questions and multi-turn interaction. An authenticated investor may create or revisit a conversation, submit a Vietnamese natural-language question, receive an answer based on available system information, and continue with additional turns. The assistant helps explain terminology and financial information, but its answers remain informational and are subject to the same data and model limitations as the main analysis.

\subsubsection{Experimentation and Backtesting Flow}

The authorized evaluator uses the experimentation web interface for controlled analysis and historical evaluation. AI-assisted analysis can be requested for an individual stock or for the VN30 or VN100 universe. Backtesting can be executed for an individual stock or sequentially for the VN30 universe. The evaluator can monitor batch progress and inspect or export the resulting reports. A completed backtest presents price charts, generated entries and exits, performance and risk measures, and relevant analytical results.
