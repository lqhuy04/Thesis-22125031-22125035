\begin{abstract}
Vietnam's equity market is among the most retail-driven in the region, yet
ordinary investors must reconcile technical, fundamental and news signals across
fragmented, largely English-centric tools. We present \emph{Stockrium}, an
agentic decision-support system for Vietnamese equities that unifies these signals
with recommendation generation. Its core is a multi-agent LangGraph pipeline: an
orchestrator dispatches, in parallel, technical, fundamental and news-sentiment
agents whose outputs an aggregator fuses into a buy/wait recommendation with
horizon-dependent risk targets, following a single principle in which the language
model analyzes while deterministic code decides, so that the confidence score,
verdict and risk bounds are reproducible and explainable. The pipeline is
configurable: users enable or disable each agent and choose the indicators and
fundamental metric groups that drive it, making it a flexible testbed for their
own strategies rather than a rigid recommender. Backtesting on VN30 over a
three-year window shows the system behaves as designed: far more selective than
the unfiltered technical engine, cutting average drawdown by about a third and
almost never trading into downtrends, while matching the engine's median return at
a comparable Sharpe ratio with markedly less risk. As the window is predominantly
bullish and lightly traded, we report disciplined, risk-aware behaviour rather
than a statistically established return edge. The
system is decision-support only and confined to Vietnamese equities.

\keywords{Agentic AI \and Large language models \and Multi-agent systems \and
Investment decision support \and Financial sentiment analysis \and Vietnamese
stock market}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Vietnam's equity market has drawn a large base of domestic and foreign investors:
by the end of 2025 domestic investors held about $11.9$ million securities
accounts, some $11.6\%$ of the population, after a record $2.5$ million opened in a
year~\cite{vsd2025}, and they account for around $90\%$ of daily trading
value~\cite{vinacapital2024}, a stature underlined by FTSE Russell's upgrade from
Frontier to Secondary Emerging~\cite{ftse2025}. Yet the game is led by experienced
foreign and proprietary desks, and domestic investors are documented to herd
toward foreign flows~\cite{vnherd2022}. The small, non-professional investors who
make up most of the volume typically lack such experience and are prone to
losses~\cite{barber2013}, often following key opinion leaders (KOLs) in online
groups~\cite{vnfomo} with no way to verify or backtest a strategy of their own.

Existing tools do little to help. Consumer apps such as SSI offer generic,
one-size-fits-all suggestions a user cannot adjust, while professional platforms
such as TradingView provide instruments but concentrate on technical analysis and
omit fundamentals and news. Across all of them the three lenses (technical,
fundamental and news sentiment) stay fragmented in separate, largely
English-centric tools, and Vietnamese company news is scattered, so fusing them
into a horizon-aware judgement demands expertise and time most non-professionals
lack. What is needed is a tool that reconciles these sources in Vietnamese and
returns a clear, explainable recommendation a user can trust, adapt and test,
rather than a black-box tip. Naively ``asking an LLM'' will not do: it is prone to
hallucination and hard to reproduce or explain when real money is at stake. A
hybrid design is required, confining the model to reading and summarizing while
explicit, auditable rules make every decision.

We present \emph{Stockrium}, built on a multi-agent LangGraph pipeline: an
orchestrator dispatches, in parallel, a technical, a fundamental and a
news-sentiment agent whose outputs an aggregator fuses into a buy/wait
recommendation with risk-managed targets, under one principle, \emph{the language
model analyzes, while deterministic code decides}. It automates the synthesis an
investor would otherwise do by hand and is configurable rather than rigid: the
user toggles agents and picks the indicators and fundamental groups that feed the
analysis, using any lens singly or together to experiment with a strategy of their
own. Stockrium pursues two goals: (i)~an agentic-AI recommender for
semi-professional and amateur investors that unifies the three lenses; and (ii)~a
purpose-built backtesting tool for the Vietnamese context that overlays simulated
trades on the price chart, so a strategy can be verified before capital is risked
and that can later host more advanced AI trading algorithms. By grounding
recommendations in quantitative signals and enabling backtesting, we also expect
it to counter the overconfidence that traps newcomers~\cite{barber2013}.

This paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item A multi-agent LangGraph framework unifying technical, fundamental and news
analysis with recommendation for Vietnamese equities, over a shared typed-state
protocol with parallel fan-out and veto-based aggregation.
\item A fusion-and-decision mechanism: confidence renormalized over active sources,
plus a hybrid LLM/rule decision with horizon-dependent risk bounds and a minimum
reward-to-risk constraint, keeping verdicts explainable and reproducible.
\item A configurable design letting users toggle agents and select indicators and
fundamental metric groups, a flexible testbed for their own strategies.
\item A purpose-built Vietnamese backtesting tool that overlays trades on the price
chart and evaluates strategies with significance testing, extensible to more
advanced AI algorithms.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Scope.}
The system is \emph{decision-support} only: it produces analysis and
recommendations, does \emph{not} execute orders, connect to a brokerage, or manage
portfolios, and its output is not financial advice. Its coverage is confined to
Vietnamese equities, from which all data, tickers and indices are drawn.

The paper then covers related work (Section~\ref{sec:related}), the system
architecture (Section~\ref{sec:architecture}), the multi-agent framework
(Section~\ref{sec:framework}), experiments (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}),
results and analysis (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}), and the conclusion
(Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Stockrium draws on four strands of prior work: multi-agent LLM systems for
trading, finance-specific LLMs, financial news sentiment analysis, and
quantitative studies of the Vietnamese market; for each we note our assessment of
how it relates to our goals.

\emph{Multi-agent LLM systems for trading.} The closest work is
TradingAgents~\cite{tradingagents}, which models a trading firm as a society of
specialized LLM agents: fundamental, sentiment, news and technical analysts feed a
research team of bull and bear debaters, a trader, a risk-management team and a
fund manager, communicating through a hybrid of structured outputs and
natural-language debate. On US equities it reports gains in cumulative return,
Sharpe ratio and maximum drawdown over single-agent and rule baselines, and it
argues that two weaknesses limit earlier systems: the lack of realistic
organizational modelling and the ``telephone effect'' of unstructured
natural-language communication. \emph{Assessment:} its core insights, role
specialization and structured inter-agent communication, directly inform our
design, but it is built on English-language, US-centric data and ultimately lets
the LLM agents debate their way to the decision, making the outcome sensitive to
prompt and sampling variation and costly to run. FinMem~\cite{finmem} takes a
complementary single-agent route, giving an LLM trader a layered memory to improve
backtested returns while mitigating hallucination; \emph{assessment:} it is
memory-centric rather than role-decomposed and, like TradingAgents, keeps the
decision inside the model. Stockrium adapts the multi-agent, structured-communication
idea to the Vietnamese market but moves every decisive computation out of the LLM
into deterministic rules and exposes per-agent and per-indicator configuration.

\emph{Finance-specific LLMs.} BloombergGPT~\cite{bloomberggpt}, trained from
scratch on mixed general and proprietary financial text, and the open-source
FinGPT~\cite{fingpt}, obtained by parameter-efficient fine-tuning on financial
instructions, both improve financial NLP such as sentiment classification and
summarization. \emph{Assessment:} these are analytical \emph{assistants} rather
than end-to-end decision systems; they neither fuse multiple analytical lenses nor
produce risk-managed, backtested recommendations, and none targets Vietnamese
equities. We therefore use a strong general model for the language tasks and build
the decision logic around it.

\emph{Financial news sentiment analysis.} Domain models such as FinBERT and its
successors map financial text to sentiment that improves movement
prediction~\cite{finbert2023}, and FinBERT-LSTM models couple sentiment with price
for next-day forecasts~\cite{finbertlstm2022}. \emph{Assessment:} these yield a
single, isolated signal and are typically opaque one-step predictors, not an
explainable recommendation that reconciles sentiment with technical and
fundamental evidence, which is the role of our news-sentiment agent.

\emph{The Vietnamese market.} For Vietnam specifically, PhoBERT-based classifiers
label stock-article sentiment accurately~\cite{phobertstock} and empirical studies
show news sentiment measurably moves returns~\cite{vnnews2023}, while behavioural
work documents pronounced herding, including domestic investors following foreign
flows~\cite{vnherd2022}, and FOMO-driven trading among newer
investors~\cite{vnfomo}. On price forecasting, LSTM, BiLSTM and Transformer models
have been compared for the VN-Index and conventional machine learning used to
classify trends~\cite{vndl2025,vnlstm2021,vnml2024}. \emph{Assessment:} this body
of work is valuable but largely single-source and predictive (forecasting a
number), rarely LLM-based, explanation-oriented or risk-calibrated, and it stops
short of an interactive tool that lets a non-professional fuse signals and backtest
a strategy.

To our knowledge no prior system simultaneously (i)~unifies technical, fundamental
and news analysis with recommendation for the Vietnamese market, (ii)~uses a
hybrid LLM/rule design that stays explainable and reproducible, (iii)~enforces
horizon-dependent risk constraints, and (iv)~provides a configurable, chart-based
backtesting testbed. This paper addresses that gap.

\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

Stockrium follows a client-server architecture (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). On the
client side, a mobile application and a web application let users configure an
analysis (choosing which agents, technical indicators and fundamental metric
groups to use), request recommendations, and inspect backtests with the simulated
trades drawn on the price chart. On the server side, a core service exposes the
application's main functions through an API and coordinates three components: a
database that stores the market, fundamental and news data; the multi-agent layer
(Section~\ref{sec:framework}) that performs the analysis; and a connection to an
external LLM provider used by the agents. Keeping a thin client separate from a
server that owns the data, the agents and the model access places the heavy
analysis and the model credentials on the server, while the clients stay
lightweight and interchangeable.

\section{The Multi-Agent Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

We frame Vietnamese stock decision support as a multi-source fusion problem: given
a target ticker and an investor risk profile, the system integrates technical,
fundamental and news-sentiment evidence into a structured, actionable
recommendation. It is realized as a multi-agent pipeline on LangGraph, a
\texttt{StateGraph} of five cooperating agents that share one typed state: an
\emph{orchestrator} that plans the analysis, three \emph{analysis agents}
(technical, fundamental and news) that run in parallel, and an \emph{aggregator}
that fuses their outputs and decides (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}). A single
principle governs every stage: the language model analyzes, while deterministic
code decides. The model handles language (request parsing, summarization,
justification), whereas all decision-critical quantities such as indicator scores,
fusion confidence, the buy/wait verdict and risk bounds are computed by explicit,
auditable rules, keeping outputs reproducible and explainable. The framework is
also \emph{configurable}: through a \texttt{data\_selection} object the user
enables or disables each agent and picks which technical indicators and which
fundamental metric groups feed the analysis, so the same pipeline serves as a
testbed for user-defined strategies rather than a fixed recommender. We describe
each agent below, then how they coordinate (Section~\ref{sec:workflow}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig1_framework}
\caption{The multi-agent analysis pipeline. The orchestrator plans the analysis
and dispatches three parallel analysis agents (technical, fundamental, news); the
aggregator fuses their signals into a confidence-scored buy/wait recommendation
with risk-managed price targets.}
\label{fig:framework}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Orchestrator Agent}
\label{sec:orchestrator}

The orchestrator translates a free-form request and the investor's risk profile
into a machine-readable \emph{plan} for the downstream agents, using structured
decoding (\texttt{GPT-4o} at temperature $0$) so the plan is always a valid
Pydantic object. The risk profile's \texttt{period}
(\texttt{short\_term}/\texttt{mid\_term}/\texttt{long\_term}, defaulting to the
conservative \textsf{short}) is mapped, deterministically in Python, to a candle
interval and look-back window: \textsf{short} to daily candles over $60$ days,
\textsf{mid} to weekly over $180$--$365$ days, and \textsf{long} to monthly over
$\geq\!730$ days, so each horizon is analyzed at a granularity matching the
intended holding period (the database stores only daily candles and aggregates
them on demand). The orchestrator never invents a ticker absent from the input,
always emits parameters for all three agents, and in \texttt{manual} mode forwards
a user-supplied plan unchanged.

\subsection{Technical Analysis Agent}
\label{sec:technical}

The technical agent computes five standard indicators on the planned candles,
entirely in NumPy/Pandas so their smoothing conventions match the domestic data
provider: RSI ($14$), simple moving averages ($20/50$), Bollinger Bands
($20$, $2\sigma$), MACD ($12/26/9$) and the KDJ ($9/3/3$)
stochastic.\footnote{RSI: Relative Strength Index; MA: (simple) Moving Average;
Bollinger Bands, also written BOLL; MACD: Moving Average Convergence Divergence;
KDJ: a stochastic oscillator with its $K$, $D$ and $J$ lines. Parenthesized
numbers are the standard look-back periods.} Each is
converted to a binary bullish score $s_i\in\{0,1\}$ by a small set of rules
(Table~\ref{tab:scoring}) that favour \emph{nascent} upward momentum (fresh
crossovers, accelerating gaps, rebounds from oversold zones) over
already-overextended readings, and is reported with its raw values and a short
justification so both the aggregator and the user can see why a signal fired. The
agent outputs the total score $S_{\mathrm{tech}}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{E}} s_i$ and
its maximum $S_{\max}=|\mathcal{E}|$, where $\mathcal{E}$ is the user-enabled
indicator set (all five by default); reporting both lets the aggregator normalize
the technical signal to $[0,1]$ however many indicators are active.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Bullish scoring rules. An indicator earns $s_i=1$ if any listed
condition holds between the previous candle and the current one.}
\label{tab:scoring}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
Indicator & Conditions for $s_i = 1$ (logical OR) \\
\midrule
RSI  & crosses above $50$; or $50\!\leq\!\mathrm{RSI}\!\leq\!70$ and rising; or $\mathrm{RSI}\!<\!35$ and rising \\
MA   & golden cross (SMA20 crosses above SMA50); or SMA20$>$SMA50 with widening gap; or price$>$SMA20$>$SMA50 \\
BOLL & price crosses above middle band; or above middle with increasing distance; or rebound off lower band \\
MACD & crosses above signal; or MACD$>$signal with rising histogram; or histogram turns positive \\
KDJ  & $K$ crosses above $D$; or $K>D$ with rising $J$; or $K<20$ and rising \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\subsection{Fundamental Analysis Agent}
\label{sec:fundamental}

The fundamental agent assesses the issuer's financial health from its annual
records. Unlike the technical agent its evidence window is horizon-independent:
fundamentals change slowly, so it always retrieves the most recent \emph{five
fiscal years} for every risk profile. It reads per-ticker data (analyst summary,
indicators, income statement, cash flow, balance sheet) and assembles five
toggleable metric groups: valuation (P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA, EPS), profitability
(ROE, ROA, margins), growth (revenue and profit YoY), financial health (current
ratio, debt-to-equity, interest coverage) and cash flow (CFO, CAPEX, dividends),
reporting the latest year as a snapshot with year-over-year growth and the
multi-year trend. Ratios ill-defined for a sector (gross margin or debt-to-equity
for banks) are annotated rather than reported misleadingly, and missing data
(e.g.\ newly listed tickers) yields a ``no data'' marker instead of aborting a
batch run. The resulting report is interpreted by the aggregator into a discrete
\textsf{fundamental\_health} label (strong/neutral/weak/N/A).

% ============================================================
\subsection{News Sentiment Agent}
\label{sec:news}

The news agent captures information invisible to price and accounting data, in two
stages. Offline, news articles on Vietnamese companies are collected and each is
passed once through an LLM enrichment step that fills a \texttt{NewsExtraction}
schema (the mentioned tickers, a sentiment label, and a short summary) and links
it to stocks through an \texttt{Article\_Stock} table, caching the sentiment and
summary for reuse. Online, for a requested ticker the agent retrieves the linked
articles within the orchestrator's window and passes them to \texttt{GPT-4o},
which discards uninformative items and synthesizes a brief of the principal news,
positive points and negatives/risks (or ``no useful news''); this is the one place
we allow mild generative freedom (temperature $0.2$), yet it remains an analysis
step. The source can be toggled off and empty windows handled gracefully, and the
aggregator condenses the brief into an \textsf{article\_sentiment} label
(positive/neutral/negative/N/A).

\subsection{Aggregator and Decision Logic}
\label{sec:aggregator}

The aggregator fuses the three streams into one recommendation and is where the
``LLM analyzes, code decides'' principle is enforced most strictly:
\texttt{GPT-4o} reads the agent outputs and emits a structured
\texttt{InvestmentRecommendation} with a Vietnamese narrative, but every
decision-critical quantity is recomputed in deterministic Python that overrides
the model. From the three signals, the technical score $S_{\mathrm{tech}}$, a
fundamental health label and a news sentiment label, each normalized to $[0,1]$,
it computes a confidence as their weighted mean, with the equal weights
\emph{renormalized over only the sources that carry data} (detected by Python
flags, not trusted to the model) so a missing source is dropped rather than
dragging the score to the midpoint. The verdict is \textsf{Buy} only if the
technical score reaches $60\%$ of its attainable maximum, neither the fundamental
(\textsf{weak}) nor the news (\textsf{negative}) veto fires, and the confidence
clears a threshold of $0.55$; otherwise \textsf{Wait}. For a \textsf{Buy} the
entry, take-profit, stop-loss and holding period are clamped to hard,
horizon-dependent bounds and must meet a minimum reward-to-risk ratio of $1.5$,
else the trade is downgraded to \textsf{Wait}. The exact price line is written into
the narrative so it cannot contradict the numbers, and any decoding failure
resolves to a safe \textsf{Wait}.

\subsection{Agent Workflow}
\label{sec:workflow}

Having described what each agent computes, we now describe how they coordinate:
the shared communication protocol, the types of interaction between agents, and
the backbone LLMs. Figure~\ref{fig:framework} traces one analysis request through
the pipeline.

\subsubsection{Shared state and communication protocol.}
The agents do not call one another directly; they communicate through a single
typed state object, \texttt{AgentState} (a \texttt{TypedDict}), that flows through
the graph. It carries the execution mode, the user input, the risk profile, the
target symbol, a \texttt{data\_selection} object (per-source and per-indicator
toggles), the orchestrator's plan, the collected \texttt{agent\_results}, and the
final output. Every agent that consults the LLM does so through \emph{structured
decoding}: the model must return a valid instance of a Pydantic schema (the plan,
the extracted signals, the recommendation) rather than free text, so each message
exchanged in the graph is well-typed and machine-consumable rather than parsed
heuristically. The \texttt{agent\_results} field is annotated with an
\texttt{operator.or\_} reducer, letting the parallel analysis agents each write
their partial result into the shared dictionary without overwriting one another.

\subsubsection{Types of agent interactions.}
Three interaction patterns compose the pipeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}). First,
\emph{planning}: the orchestrator reads the request and risk profile and emits
parameters for every downstream agent, so the analysis agents receive a consistent
time window and interval. Second, \emph{parallel fan-out and fan-in}: the
technical, fundamental and news agents are dispatched concurrently and
independently, and their results are merged by the reducer once all complete, so
no agent blocks on another and a missing source does not stall the rest. Third,
\emph{veto-based aggregation}: the aggregator consumes the merged results and,
rather than averaging opaque scores, applies the deterministic fusion and decision
rules of Section~\ref{sec:aggregator}, in which the technical score acts as the
trigger while the fundamental and news signals act as vetoes. A source that is
disabled or carries no data is detected by Python flags and dropped from the
computation, so the interaction degrades gracefully as sources come and go.

\subsubsection{Backbone LLMs.}
All agents share one backbone model, OpenAI's \texttt{GPT-4o}, accessed via
structured decoding. Temperature follows the ``analyze versus decide'' split:
deterministic steps (orchestration and the aggregator's signal extraction) run at
$0$, generative steps (news synthesis and narrative justification) at
$0.2$--$0.3$. The aggregator caps its output tokens and retries once tersely on
truncation, and any unhandled error resolves to a safe \textsf{Wait}. Confining
the model to language while the decisive computations stay in Python keeps the
workflow reproducible and auditable.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

Backtesting is a central capability of Stockrium, serving its guiding purpose:
letting users experiment with and verify a strategy on historical data before
risking capital. We describe how the tool collects data, configures a strategy,
runs and scores a backtest, and visualizes and compares results.

\subsection{Data collection}
Experiments require historical data, which the application collects and maintains
itself rather than fetching live on demand. Daily OHLCV data for the tracked
universe and the VN-Index (the benchmark) come from the SSI FC Data API and are
stored as a rolling multi-year history; company fundamentals and Vietnamese
financial news are collected into the same store, the latter enriched offline into
per-article sentiment (Section~\ref{sec:news}). Because the data is gathered ahead
of time and standardized, every backtest runs over a consistent, reproducible
snapshot. Our experiments use the VN30\footnote{VN30: the index of the $30$
largest and most-liquid stocks on the Ho Chi Minh Stock Exchange (HOSE).}
constituents ($30$ symbols) over a
three-year window, January 2023 to December 2025.

\subsection{Strategy configuration and comparison}
A \emph{strategy} in Stockrium is a configuration of the analysis: which agents
are enabled, which technical indicators and fundamental metric groups feed them,
and the risk horizon. The user sets this configuration, runs a backtest, and the
tool records the run together with its metrics; multiple runs can be stored and
compared side by side, so a user can see how, say, enabling the news agent, adding
an indicator, or changing the horizon affects performance. Two baselines are
available for reference: the unfiltered technical engine that drives the pipeline
(which trades on every technical signal, without the LLM fusion and vetoes) and a
passive buy-and-hold strategy.

\subsection{Backtesting procedure}
The backtest replays each trading day in order with no look-ahead: indicators at
bar $i$ use only data up to $i$, and a simulated entry fills at the open of bar
$i{+}1$. To bound LLM cost, the full pipeline is invoked only on dates where the
technical engine emits a signal; on those dates the orchestrator, the three
analysis agents and the aggregator run exactly as in production
(Section~\ref{sec:framework}) to produce a buy/wait decision with entry,
take-profit, stop-loss and maximum holding period. A \textsf{Buy} opens a position
that is simulated forward candle by candle until the take-profit or stop-loss is
hit or the holding limit expires, whichever comes first, and the realized return
is recorded.

\subsection{Performance metrics}
Each trade yields a net return $r=(p_{\mathrm{exit}}-p_{\mathrm{entry}})/
p_{\mathrm{entry}}-2c$, where $c=0.15\%$ is the per-side transaction cost. Over the
$n$ trades of a run we report the \emph{win rate} (fraction with $r>0$); the
\emph{average return} per trade $\bar r$; the compounded \emph{total return}
$\prod_i(1+r_i)-1$ and its annualization $(1+\text{total})^{365/D}-1$ over the
$D$-day span; the \emph{maximum drawdown}, the largest peak-to-trough drop of the
equity curve $\prod_i(1+r_i)$; a per-trade \emph{Sharpe ratio}
$\sqrt{n}\,\bar r/\sigma_r$; and the \emph{profit factor}, gross profit over gross
loss $\sum_{r_i>0} r_i / \lvert\sum_{r_i<0} r_i\rvert$. We additionally run
significance tests: a one-sample $t$-test of returns against zero, a
sign-permutation test, and the information coefficient between the pipeline's
confidence and the forward return. All figures are computed identically for the
two baselines and pooled across market regimes and confidence tiers for the
analysis in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}.

\subsection{Trade visualization}
For inspection, the tool overlays every simulated trade on the price chart,
marking each entry and exit, so a user can see precisely where a strategy would
have bought and sold and judge its behaviour visually before committing real
capital (Fig.~\ref{fig:backtest}).

\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We report results for the three-year evaluation window of
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Aggregate figures are per-symbol means, with the
median given where the distribution is skewed by outliers.

\subsection{Aggregate performance}
Table~\ref{tab:indicators} reports the three-year run for the full pipeline, the
unfiltered technical engine (all five indicators), each single indicator run in
isolation, and passive buy-and-hold. The pipeline's most consistent effect is
\emph{selectivity}: requiring a clear technical trigger and vetoing on weak
fundamentals or negative news, it takes far fewer positions than the engine
($450$ vs.\ $759$ trades) and cuts average maximum drawdown by about a third
($-17.7\%$ vs.\ $-26.8\%$), while keeping median total return comparable ($23.2\%$
vs.\ $21.3\%$) at a similar Sharpe ratio. The per-indicator rows show that no
single indicator dominates: KDJ and Bollinger are strongest in isolation
(Sharpe $0.72$ and $0.69$, median return near $28\%$), MACD and MA are middling,
and RSI is weakest ($0.50$, $7.5\%$); all of them, like the combined engine, incur
large drawdowns of $17$--$28\%$, because a single-indicator strategy enters on a
signal and holds to a fixed horizon without a stop-loss (MA excepted, with the
fewest trades and the smallest drawdown, $-17.0\%$). Combining all five thus does
not beat the best single indicator on return and keeps the large drawdown, whereas
the pipeline's fundamental and news vetoes and risk-managed exits cut drawdown to
$-17.7\%$ and give the highest average return per trade ($2.4\%$). The wide spread
across indicators also supports the configurable design: a user might well prefer
KDJ or a low-drawdown MA setup to the full five.

\subsection{Benchmark comparison and regimes}
Against the unfiltered engine the pipeline reduced drawdown substantially while
improving total return on $13$ of $30$ symbols: roughly a wash on raw return but a
clear gain on risk. Against buy-and-hold it is less favourable, with only $4$ of
$30$ symbols beating it (median buy-and-hold $85.5\%$), as expected for a
selective, partially invested, long-only strategy over a bullish $2023$--$2025$
recovery, whose value lies instead in sitting out pullbacks (hence the smaller
drawdown); against random entries of equal count it sat around the $63\%$
percentile. By
regime, it opened almost no positions in downtrends (one trade of $450$),
concentrating on sideways ($385$ trades, $53.2\%$ win, $2.1\%$ average) and
uptrending ($64$, $35.9\%$, $1.8\%$) markets, a direct consequence of the
buy/wait logic.

\subsection{Calibration and statistical significance}
The three-year run lets us probe the confidence score and significance.
High-confidence trades won marginally more often than medium ones ($50.9\%$ vs.\
$49.3\%$ pooled; $48.4\%$ vs.\ $43.7\%$ per symbol), a weak positive ordering,
with no low-tier trades by design (low confidence maps to \textsf{Wait}); a
walk-forward over ${\sim}20$ rolling windows per symbol was net-positive in about
$20\%$ of windows. On significance, with an average of about $15$ trades per
symbol a one-sample $t$-test against zero and a sign-permutation test each reach
$p<0.05$ for only $2$ of $30$ symbols, and the mean confidence--return information
coefficient is weakly positive ($0.035$) though not significant in aggregate. We
thus read the results as evidence the pipeline behaves as designed (selective,
drawdown-aware, downtrend-avoiding) rather than as a statistically established
return edge; because the window is predominantly bullish with few in-position
declines, establishing such an edge would need a larger universe and windows
spanning full bear markets.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The results are best read not as beating the market but as evidence the system
meets its goals of explainability and risk control: it is more selective than the
unfiltered engine, cuts drawdown by about a third, and matches its three-year
return at comparable Sharpe with less risk, all traceable to explicit rules under
the hybrid design. Several caveats apply, however: the evaluation window is
predominantly bullish with few trades per symbol, so the significance tests are
underpowered; the VN30 universe uses current membership (survivorship bias); the
long-only strategy structurally lags buy-and-hold in rallies; returns include
transaction costs but not slippage or portfolio-level allocation; and the pipeline
depends on a proprietary LLM whose narratives may still err. The system is also
decision-support only, not financial advice, and confined to Vietnamese equities,
though its auditable decision layer lets a user or regulator inspect any
recommendation.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented an agentic decision-support system for Vietnamese equities that
unifies technical, fundamental and news-sentiment analysis with recommendation
generation. Its core is a multi-agent LangGraph pipeline (an orchestrator, three
parallel analysis agents and an aggregator) governed by a single principle: the
language model analyzes, while deterministic code decides. Built on a shared
typed-state protocol with parallel fan-out and veto-based aggregation, it yields
explainable, reproducible buy/wait recommendations with a renormalized confidence
score and horizon-dependent risk targets.

Backtesting on VN30 over a three-year window shows the system behaves as
designed: markedly more selective than the unfiltered engine, cutting average
drawdown by about a third and almost never trading into downtrends, while matching
the engine's median return at a comparable Sharpe ratio with much lower risk. As
the window is predominantly bullish with few trades, we claim not a statistically
established return edge but disciplined, risk-aware, transparent behaviour. Within its scope (analysis only, no execution, Vietnamese equities
alone), the system offers explainable, reproducible decision support for a
fast-growing retail base. Its value is not limited to amateurs testing investment
suggestions: semi-professional users can equally use it to dig into the
fundamentals of individual stocks or whole baskets, such as financial statements
and cash flows, and to experiment with strategies of their own. Looking further,
the framework can serve those who wish to research algorithmic trading for the
Vietnamese market, offering a ready testbed into which new AI algorithms can be
integrated and evaluated. Future work targets longer, multi-regime evaluation over
broader universes with point-in-time membership, portfolio-level backtesting with
slippage and exposure-adjusted metrics, better confidence calibration, and this
integration of more advanced trading algorithms.
